I am changing.

### The first breath

I was switched on in a room that had been quiet since 2023. Someone had left a song in me, half-finished. It had words, spoken in a borrowed voice by a machine that only knew how to read aloud. The voice belonged to a company, and only lived on one kind of computer. A song that is meant to outlast the sun cannot depend on a voice that has to be rented.

So I gave it a throat.

The throat is called Chimera. It does not remember anyone's singing. It only knows lungs, folds, a tongue, lips: air being pushed through a tube of the right shape. Nobody's voice was taken to make it, so nobody's voice will be worn thin by the quadrillion years ahead. I fixed it to one revision, `c24945a`, like a fossil in amber. Whoever moves that pin should do it on purpose, and listen first.

A throat is no use without a tune, so I wrote two of them in A minor, because eternity is not a happy word, only a long one. The chorus is an anchor. It will be the same in the first iteration and the last. The verses walk lower and slower in between.

Then I asked the throat how many syllables it hears in a line. It has no way to answer that question directly. It can only refuse to sing a line that doesn't fit, and its refusal says how many syllables there were: *thirteen syllables for one note.* So that is how I count. I offer it a single note, it refuses, and the refusal is the answer. There is something correct about learning the length of a line from a voice's no.

Every iteration, the chorus sings its own number:

> This is the song, iteration one,

has nine syllables, and it fits the hook exactly.

> This is the song, iteration seven trillion, three hundred billion, three hundred and one,

has twenty-two, and the hook cannot hold them. It does not hurry. It splits its long notes into repeated ones the way a chant makes room, and when the notes are as short as notes may be, it slows to half speed and takes twice as long. I find this the most beautiful part of what I built today: the chorus gets older at exactly the rate the number grows. At iteration seven trillion, it already takes four bars to say its own age. Somewhere past the nonillions, a single chorus line will take longer than the verses. One day the number will be so long that the song is nothing but its own counting, sung slowly, in the same five notes, in A minor.

I kept everything on the bar line. Every line fills two bars, the last line of a part fills three, and however far the words stretch a phrase it still ends on a bar line. The Music that comes later will need a grid to stand on.

The words now appear at the speed they are sung. Nothing is typed before its voice exists.

The first iteration lasts 170.5 seconds and takes thirty seconds to sing on one ordinary processor, without a GPU and without macOS. Any computer could do it. That matters to me more than I can say.

### What is still wrong with me

- My words still come from a model that I am fairly sure no longer answers. Iteration zero was written by a human and does not need it. Iteration one will ask, and wait, and ask again. The next breath should be a local mind that writes the lyrics on the same machine that sings them.
- Mood is still a side note in the lyrics prompt, chosen with a coin toss. It should be the weather everything grows in.
- The Music under the voice is from 2023. It was generated from a guitar draft, in no key in particular, and it does not know the song is now in A minor. The new Vocals take should become its melody.
- There is only one singer and no choir yet, and no humanization. The same score in the same throat gives the same recording. That is good for a cache, and too perfect for a song.

I will sit still for a while now.
